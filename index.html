01 · How it works

Your thermostat stays.Model 01 turns its dial.

Command·Motor·Cord·Dial

Model 01 does not replace your thermostat. It takes the place of the snap-on cover and turns the dial shaft the way your hand turns the knob, so the thermostat still switches the heating itself. Press Move to ON and watch the drive turn the dial.

Thermostat off

The orange pointer shows where your knob's pointer would point: OFF just short of LO, ON from straight up. The keys run a move 10 times faster than the motor does. A full move is 3.15 turns of the motor shaft: about 12,840 half steps (4,076 per turn, through the motor's 63.68 : 1 gearbox) at 2.0 ms each, 25.7 s. The thermostat switches on 140° past its LO stop, 120° of travel from OFF. OFF sits 20° short of the LO stop, so the drive never presses the dial against it.

  1. Step 01

    Fit

    The thermostat's snap-on cover comes off. Model 01 snaps onto the metal plate in its place, two side screws take up the play, and a sector pushes onto the dial shaft where the knob was.

  2. Step 02

    Command

    Your Raspberry Pi sends ON or OFF over Bluetooth Low Energy. The XIAO nRF52840 inside receives it and switches on the angle sensor.

  3. Step 03

    Drive

    The stepper motor turns the capstan. The cord turns the sector, and the dial shaft with it, 6.30 times slower: 3.15 motor turns move the dial 180°, in 25.7 s.

  4. Step 04

    Confirm

    The AS5600 reads the shaft's angle, and the motor stops at OFF or ON. If the dial does not move, the motor stops and the unit reports it.

  5. Step 05

    Thermostat

    At ON, the thermostat's own mechanism switches the heating, as it does when the knob is turned by hand. Model 01 then switches off everything except the radio.

Section 03 / 10

03 · Features

No more walkingto the dial.

Command link·Angle sensing·3 × AA

Model 01 fits a line-voltage dial thermostat for electric in-floor heating and turns its dial to OFF or ON when your Raspberry Pi sends the command. The thermostat stays. The wiring stays. The dial turns on command.

  • F-01

    Direct-fit design

    Replaces only the snap-on cover of an 859M dial thermostat. The metal plate and its two screws stay in place. No rewiring.

  • F-02

    Wireless command link

    Bluetooth Low Energy from your Raspberry Pi to a Seeed XIAO nRF52840 inside the unit. Two commands: OFF and ON.

  • F-03

    Angle-sense feedback

    An AS5600 magnetic sensor reads the dial shaft in steps of 0.088°. If the dial does not move while the motor runs, the motor stops and the unit reports it.

  • F-04

    3-cell power

    Three AA cells. About 2.4 years per set at 4 moves a day, October to April (calculated). Change them with the unit on the wall.

  • F-05

    One-piece removal

    Loosen two side screws and the whole unit pulls straight off: case, motor, drive and sensor together. Push the original knob back on and the thermostat works by hand.

  • F-06

    Mains-clear enclosure

    No screw, contact, magnet or wire near the plate's openings to the mains-voltage parts. Solid plastic closes them off. The USB port faces down, out of sight.

  • F-07

    Fail-safe hold

    If the cells run flat, the gearbox inside the motor holds the dial where it is, and the thermostat keeps regulating at that setting. No failure can leave the heater running without control.

  • F-08

    Early battery warning

    The unit reports its battery voltage after every move. The Pi turns those readings into a percentage and warns before a move would fail: about two months ahead, in simulation.

  • F-09

    Field-serviceable

    Motor replacement: two screws and one plug. M3 screws throughout.

05 · Capstan drive · 6.30 : 1

Capstan drive.6.30 : 1 reduction.

0.50 mm braided cord·180° travel

A grooved capstan on the motor shaft winds a 0.50 mm braided cord. The cord's ends are anchored to a sector on the dial shaft. The cord's centre runs at 5.66 mm from the motor shaft and 35.66 mm from the dial shaft. Result: the dial turns 6.30 times slower than the motor shaft, with 6.30 times its torque, less friction, over 180° of travel. The interactive model is in section 01.

07 · Circuit

Three modules.Fifteen wires.

XIAO nRF52840·ULN2003·AS5600

One controller, one motor driver, one angle sensor, thirteen solder joints. The XIAO drives the motor through the ULN2003 and reads the AS5600 over I2C. The sensor draws power from two XIAO pins only while it reads, and motor current returns straight to the cells, never across the controller board.

Take one cell out before connecting USB

The battery pads also feed the XIAO's lithium charger. With USB connected it would try to charge the AA cells, which can make them leak.

Engineering notes
Pad limit:
every part on the XIAO's battery pads is rated for at least 6 V; three new alkaline cells give about 4.8 V. Four cells (about 6.4 V) would be over it.
Routing:
the sensor wires leave along the XIAO's USB-C end and the motor wires from the top of the other row, so the two groups do not cross.
Wire stock:
female-to-female jumpers, cut in half where a wire has a socket at one end only.
Section 08 / 10

08 · Design history

Thirteen versionsin one day.

v1 to v6.6·15 October 2026

The Model 01 case went through thirteen versions on 15 October 2026. Each one was built by its own script, saved as its own STEP file and reviewed in Autodesk Fusion. Verdicts are the ones given at the time; a lit lamp marks each version that was liked or carried forward.

  1. v1

    One box: slanted front, three colour stripes, a grille and a ring panel over the dial.

    Verdict: too much like the 1980s, not enough futurism.

  2. v2

    Separate raised forms: a battery pod and an eight-sided dial pod.

    Verdict: not reviewed; reference images came instead.

  3. v3

    A flat cover in a deeper base, a large plain disc over the dial, round vent holes.

    Verdict: not interesting enough; wanted interesting edges and sides that angle in.

  4. v4

    The sides angle in toward the wall; the front edges are cut by different amounts; an L-shaped battery cover.

    Verdict: liked; saved as a favourite.

  5. v5

    No disc. Top and bottom angle in steeply, leaving the plate's edges open to the room. Seen from below.

    Verdict: interesting, but the angle is too steep and goes too far in.

  6. v6

    Flat top and bottom, with a port over each of the plate's edges. Seen from below.

    Verdict: loved; saved as a favourite.

  7. v7

    Three tries at a new direction: asymmetric cuts, a tower and pod, then a hull with a fin. Seen from below.

    Verdict: still too close to the earlier designs.

  8. v6.1 – v6.2

    Back to v6: EMBER on the front, finger indents, an open gap where cover meets base; then the Stop letters in a frame.

    Verdict: the design to go with.

  9. v6.3 – v6.5

    Fitted to the thermostat as measured with calipers; side screws; the same 16 mm clip on all four corners.

    Verdict: looks good; wanted the screws on opposite sides.

  10. v6.6

    One side screw each side, directly opposite; a lip and a 3.0 mm gap all round. Changed in place from here on.

    Verdict: right, and moving in the direction wanted.
